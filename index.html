About

Bridging microbial ecology and plant science

I am a microbial ecologist and computational biologist currently working as a postdoctoral researcher at the Max Planck Institute of Colloids and Interfaces, and as a Guest Scientist at the Leibniz Institute for Agricultural Engineering and Bioeconomy (ATB), Potsdam. My work focuses on the ecological mechanisms underlying plant–microbiome interactions and their role in soil health, plant resilience, and sustainable agricultural systems.

Before this, I spent over two years at the University of Western Australia's School of Agriculture and Environment, working within the Soil Science Challenge as a microbial ecologist studying plant–microbe interactions under soil organic and biological amendments — running pot and field trials, handling bioinformatics and statistical analysis, and supervising Master's and PhD students.

I completed my PhD at the Hawkesbury Institute for the Environment, Western Sydney University, where my doctoral research explored the drivers and mechanisms of plant microbiome assembly — integrating microbial ecology, plant biology, and computational approaches across glasshouse and large-scale field experiments spanning eucalypts to native and exotic Australian grasses.

I'm always keen to engage in collaborative research across disciplines and institutions.

Research focus

What I study

Plant–microbiome interactions

How host plants select, recruit, and shape the microbial communities living in and around them, across species from eucalypts to cereal crops.

Soil and rhizosphere microbiome ecology

Tracing how soil organic amendments — biochar, compost, digestate-based products — reshape microbial communities and soil function over time.

Microbiome-mediated stress tolerance

Identifying how microbial partners help plants withstand drought, heat, and other abiotic stress, and what that means for resilient agriculture.

Functional and taxonomic profiling

Applying amplicon sequencing, shotgun metagenomics, and transcriptomics to characterise microbial communities at both taxonomic and functional resolution.

Integrating ecology with molecular biology

Combining microbial ecology with plant physiological and molecular responses to build a mechanistic picture of host–microbiome relationships.
